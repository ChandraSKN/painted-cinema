# Directing a painted-cinema film

- **Hook in the first 3 seconds.** Open on a face, a light or an action, not a skyline. The first shot paints itself in from a bare canvas — start it on the most important thing in the frame.
- **One story turn.** Setup → something changes → an ending that echoes the opening or lands a hook. Cut what doesn't serve it.
- **Every shot moves.** A slow push for pressure, a pull-back for a reveal, a tilt up for discovery, a drift sideways to observe. Keep zoom between 1.0 and ~1.35; vary the move from shot to shot.
- **Use variants for the key moments only** — one or two per film (a look, a door, a light). Too many and nothing stands out.
- **Paint-over vs cut.** Paint-over (default) for time passing or a change of place; a hard cut for shock, speed or a line of dialogue landing.
- **Light as an event.** Put `"light": "rake"` on the emotional peak: the paint ridges flare as the light drops low.
- **Pace for reading.** Each subtitle at least 1.8 s, and longer than the spoken line + 0.6 s. Hold the title card ≥ 4 s.
- **Sound.** If the user has no soundtrack, the film still works silent with subtitles; offer to mix their voice/music files with ffmpeg (voice ~10 dB above music, music ducked under lines) into one `soundtrack.m4a`.
- **Check at phone size.** If a face or prop doesn't read in a 300 px-tall preview, reframe or regenerate.
